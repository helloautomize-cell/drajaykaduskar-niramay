Does Gestational Diabetes Go Away After Delivery?
Written by
Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar
,
Diabetologist
, Nagpur
Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar
MD (Medicine), PGDHSc (Diabetology), FEACD (The Netherlands)
Published
18 October 2026
·
4 min read
Quick answer
In most women, gestational diabetes goes away within hours to days after delivery, once the placenta, which makes insulin-blocking hormones, is gone. However, some women continue to have high sugar, and all women who had it have a higher lifetime risk of type 2 diabetes. A blood sugar test at 6 to 12 weeks after delivery, and regular testing afterwards, are essential.
After months of watching your diet, checking sugars and attending visits, the big question is whether it ends with the birth. The short answer is that, for most women, blood sugar returns to normal soon after delivery. But the story does not end there.
Why does it usually settle?
During pregnancy, the placenta makes hormones that make the body more resistant to insulin. If the pancreas cannot make enough extra insulin to compensate, sugars rise. After delivery, these hormones drop quickly, and insulin sensitivity improves. Sugar levels in most women return to normal within days.
What happens in the first days after birth?
If you took insulin or tablets in pregnancy, they are usually stopped right after delivery.
Your sugar is checked in hospital, often fasting on the first day or two.
If sugars remain very high, you may have had diabetes before pregnancy, and treatment continues.
Your baby's sugar is also monitored, since babies of mothers with gestational diabetes can have low sugar after birth.
When should you be tested again?
6 to 12 weeks after delivery:
A 75 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) is recommended to see if diabetes or prediabetes persists. Fasting sugar and HbA1c may also be done, though OGTT is preferred in many guidelines.
Every 1 to 3 years:
For life, even if the first test is normal, because the risk of type 2 diabetes remains elevated.
Before the next pregnancy:
To check your sugar and plan.
How high is the risk of Type 2 diabetes?
Women who have had gestational diabetes have a much higher risk of developing type 2 diabetes later, with studies suggesting a risk several times greater than other women. In India, where the baseline risk is already high, this is especially important. The risk is highest in those who needed insulin, had high sugars early in pregnancy, were overweight or have a family history.
How can you lower your risk?
Return to a healthy weight gradually after delivery.
Follow a balanced diet with limited sweets and refined foods.
Exercise at least 150 minutes a week.
Breastfeed if possible, as it may help improve sugar metabolism.
Do not smoke.
Get sleep when you can.
Attend follow-up tests.
Discuss medicines such as metformin with your doctor if your risk is very high.
What about future pregnancies?
Gestational diabetes tends to recur in later pregnancies. Plan early testing and monitor sugars. Entering the next pregnancy at a healthy weight lowers the chance.
What about your baby?
Children of mothers with gestational diabetes may have a higher risk of obesity and diabetes later in life. Breastfeeding, a healthy diet and an active lifestyle for the child help.
When should you see a doctor?
See your doctor if after delivery you have excessive thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, blurred vision or repeated infections. Do not wait for the 6 to 12 week visit if you have symptoms.
A quick recap
Gestational diabetes usually resolves soon after delivery.
Test for diabetes 6 to 12 weeks after birth.
Lifelong screening every 1 to 3 years is needed.
Healthy weight, activity and breastfeeding lower your risk.
Plan for future pregnancies with early testing.
Your pregnancy gave you an early warning. Use it to protect your health for years to come. For a follow-up plan, consult Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar, a diabetologist at Niramay Clinics in Nagpur.

Frequently asked questions
Does gestational diabetes go away after delivery?
In most women, yes. Sugar levels return to normal within days after the placenta is delivered. But the risk of type 2 diabetes remains higher, and testing after birth is essential.
When should I get tested after delivery?
Get a glucose tolerance test 6 to 12 weeks after delivery. Then test every 1 to 3 years, or as your doctor advises.
Can I breastfeed with gestational diabetes?
Yes. Breastfeeding is encouraged. It benefits the baby and may help the mother's sugar metabolism and weight loss.
Will I get diabetes later if I had gestational diabetes?
Your risk is higher than average. Many women do not develop diabetes, especially with healthy weight, regular activity and good diet. Regular testing catches problems early.
Do I need medicines after delivery?
Usually not. Insulin and tablets used during pregnancy are stopped. If sugars remain high, your doctor will decide on treatment.
Will gestational diabetes come back in my next pregnancy?
It can. The chance is higher if you gained weight or needed insulin before. Early testing and a healthy lifestyle before and during pregnancy help.
