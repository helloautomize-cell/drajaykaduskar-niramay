When Should You See a Diabetologist?

Quick answer
See a diabetologist when diabetes is newly diagnosed, blood sugar is not controlled despite treatment, you need insulin or injectable medicines, you have complications, you are pregnant or planning pregnancy, or you have frequent low sugar episodes. Regular specialist review is also useful once a year even when control is good.
A diabetologist is a doctor who specialises in diabetes and related conditions. Not everyone needs one at first, but certain situations make specialist care valuable.
Situations that call for a specialist
Newly diagnosed diabetes, to set the right plan
HbA1c above target despite medicines
Starting or adjusting insulin
Frequent hypoglycaemia
Diabetes with kidney, eye, nerve or heart complications
Pregnancy or planning pregnancy
Diabetes in a young person or suspected type 1 diabetes
Considering newer medicines or glucose monitors
Diabetes with obesity or other metabolic problems
What a diabetologist offers
Detailed assessment and tailored treatment
Guidance on food, activity and monitoring
Screening for complications
Experience with newer medicines and technology
How to prepare
Bring recent reports and medicines
Bring glucose readings
Write down your questions
Working with your family doctor
Specialist and family doctor care can work together for continuity.
The quick version
Specialist care helps at diagnosis and when control slips.
Insulin, complications and pregnancy need expert input.
Yearly review is valuable.
Bring records to the visit.
Team care gives the best results.
Good diabetes care is often a team effort. For specialist guidance, you can consult Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar at Niramay Clinics in Nagpur.
When to seek urgent care
Seek urgent care for extreme thirst with vomiting, confusion, deep rapid breathing, unusual drowsiness, or signs of very low sugar such as sweating, shaking and confusion.

Frequently asked questions
Do I need a diabetologist if my sugar is controlled?
An annual review can still be helpful.
What is the difference between a diabetologist and an endocrinologist?
A diabetologist focuses on diabetes, while an endocrinologist treats hormone disorders more broadly.
Can I see one only for a second opinion?
Yes, that is common and reasonable.
What tests will they ask for?
Typically HbA1c, kidney and lipid tests, and eye and foot checks.
Will I always need insulin?
Not necessarily. Your doctor will explain your options.
How often should I visit?
Often every three to six months, or as advised.
