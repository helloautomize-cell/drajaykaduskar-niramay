Does Everyone With Type 2 Diabetes Need a CGM?

Quick answer
No. CGM is most useful for people on insulin, those with frequent low sugar, unpredictable readings or pregnancy, and anyone who needs detailed information to adjust treatment. Many people with stable type 2 diabetes on tablets do well with regular fingerstick checks and HbA1c. Your doctor can advise whether a CGM would help you.
A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) tracks sugar levels through the day and night using a small sensor worn on the body. It is a powerful tool, but not every person needs one.
Who benefits most
People on multiple daily insulin injections
Those with frequent or unnoticed low sugar
Pregnancy with diabetes
Unexplained swings in readings
People who want to understand how food and activity affect sugar, with medical guidance
Who may manage without
Stable type 2 diabetes on tablets with good HbA1c
Those who prefer simple monitoring
People for whom cost is a barrier
What a CGM shows
Trends through the day and night
Time spent in target range
Alerts for high and low sugar
Limits to know
Readings may lag slightly behind blood glucose
Sensors cost money and need replacing
Data needs interpretation, and more numbers can cause anxiety in some people
Fingerstick checks may still be needed
Getting value from a CGM
Review reports with your doctor
Focus on patterns, not single readings
Use it to guide meals, activity and medicines
A quick recap
CGM is helpful but not essential for everyone.
Insulin users benefit most.
Many stable patients manage with fingersticks.
Data needs interpretation.
Discuss with your doctor.
The best monitoring method is one that fits your treatment and lifestyle. For advice, you can consult Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar at Niramay Clinics in Nagpur.
When to seek urgent care
Seek urgent care if a sensor or meter shows very high or very low readings with symptoms such as vomiting, confusion, unusual drowsiness, sweating, shaking or fainting.

Frequently asked questions
Is CGM better than a glucometer?
It gives more information, but both are useful.
Can I use CGM for a short period?
Yes. Some people use it for a couple of weeks to learn patterns.
Does CGM replace HbA1c?
No. HbA1c is still used for long-term control.
Is it painful?
Most people feel only a brief sensation when applying the sensor.
Is CGM covered by insurance?
Coverage varies. Check with your insurer.
Can CGM help me lose weight?
It can show how foods affect sugar, but it is not a weight loss tool on its own.
