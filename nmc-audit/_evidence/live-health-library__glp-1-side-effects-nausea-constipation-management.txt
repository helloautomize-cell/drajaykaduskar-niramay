GLP-1 Side Effects: Nausea, Constipation and Fixes

Quick answer
The most common side effects of GLP-1 medicines are nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, bloating and reduced appetite. They are most likely when starting or increasing the dose and usually improve within days to weeks. Eating smaller meals, avoiding fatty food, drinking fluids and increasing the dose slowly can help. Severe stomach pain or persistent vomiting needs urgent medical care.
GLP-1 medicines are effective, but many people feel some stomach upset when they start. This is common, usually mild and often settles with time and small changes in eating habits. Knowing what to expect helps you stay on treatment safely.
Why do these medicines cause stomach symptoms?
GLP-1 medicines slow the emptying of the stomach and act on appetite centres in the brain. Food stays in the stomach longer, so you feel full sooner. This is part of how they work, but it can cause nausea, bloating and reflux, especially with large or fatty meals.
What are the common side effects?
Nausea
Vomiting
Diarrhoea
Constipation
Stomach pain or cramps
Burping, bloating and acid reflux
Tiredness and dizziness
Mild redness or itching at the injection site
How can you manage nausea and vomiting?
Eat small, frequent meals instead of large ones.
Stop eating when you feel full.
Avoid fried, oily and very sweet food.
Eat slowly and sit upright for a while after meals.
Avoid lying down right after eating.
Sip water or buttermilk through the day.
Try ginger tea or dry snacks like plain biscuits or toast when nauseated.
Avoid alcohol and strong smells that trigger nausea.
Ask your doctor about anti-nausea medicine if needed.
How can you manage constipation?
Drink enough water, 2 to 3 litres a day unless advised otherwise.
Add fibre slowly: vegetables, fruits, oats, pulses and whole grains.
Walk daily.
Ask your doctor about a mild stool softener or fibre supplement.
How can you manage diarrhoea?
Drink oral rehydration solution or fluids.
Eat light food such as curd rice, banana or khichdi.
Avoid oily, spicy foods and excess caffeine.
Inform your doctor if it lasts more than two days.
How does the dose affect side effects?
Doctors usually start with a low dose and increase every few weeks. This gives the body time to adjust. If side effects are strong, the dose may be held or reduced for a while. Do not increase the dose faster than advised.
What about serious side effects?
Rare but important problems include:
Pancreatitis:
Severe, persistent stomach pain that may spread to the back, often with vomiting.
Gallbladder problems:
Pain in the upper right abdomen, fever or yellow eyes. Rapid weight loss raises the risk of gallstones.
Dehydration and kidney strain:
From repeated vomiting or diarrhoea.
Low blood sugar:
More likely when combined with insulin or sulfonylurea tablets.
Allergic reactions:
Swelling, rash or difficulty breathing.
Thyroid concerns:
A lump in the neck, hoarseness or trouble swallowing. These medicines are not advised in people with certain thyroid cancer histories.
Eye changes:
Sudden vision changes, especially in people with diabetic eye disease.
When should you see a doctor urgently?
Seek medical care at once for severe or constant stomach pain, repeated vomiting, inability to drink fluids, signs of dehydration, yellowing of the skin or eyes, swelling of the face or throat, or a fast heartbeat with dizziness.
A quick recap
Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and constipation are the most common side effects.
They are usually mild and settle with time.
Small meals, fluids, fibre and slow dose increases help.
Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting or allergic signs need urgent care.
Never stop or change doses without speaking to your doctor.
Most people tolerate GLP-1 medicines well when they are started slowly and supported with simple food changes. Speak up early if something feels wrong. For safe supervision and dose planning, consult Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar, a diabetologist at Niramay Clinics in Nagpur.

Frequently asked questions
How long do GLP-1 side effects last?
Stomach side effects usually appear in the first weeks or after a dose increase and improve over days to weeks. If they last longer or are severe, talk to your doctor about adjusting the dose.
What should I eat when I feel nauseated on a GLP-1?
Try small portions of light food such as curd rice, toast, banana, khichdi or plain biscuits. Avoid fried, oily and sweet items. Sip fluids slowly through the day.
Can GLP-1 medicines cause constipation?
Yes. Slow stomach and gut movement can cause constipation. Drink water, add fibre gradually, stay active and speak to your doctor if it continues. A stool softener may be advised.
Do GLP-1 injections cause pancreatitis?
Pancreatitis is a rare but serious risk. Severe, persistent upper abdominal pain, sometimes spreading to the back with vomiting, needs urgent medical attention. Stop the medicine and call your doctor.
Can I take anti-nausea medicine with GLP-1?
Your doctor may prescribe a suitable anti-nausea medicine. Do not self-medicate, especially if you have other conditions or take other medicines.
Should I stop my GLP-1 medicine if I get side effects?
Do not stop on your own. Mild effects often improve. Your doctor may pause or reduce the dose, or change the plan. Stop and seek urgent help if you have severe abdominal pain or allergic signs.
