Prediabetes Diet: What to Eat in an Indian Diet

Quick answer
For prediabetes, fill half your plate with vegetables, a quarter with protein such as dal, paneer, eggs, fish or chicken, and a quarter with whole grains or millets. Limit sugary drinks, sweets, refined flour (maida) products, fried snacks and large portions of white rice. Eat regular meals, include fibre and watch portion sizes.
A prediabetes diet is an eating pattern that helps bring blood sugar back toward normal and lowers the risk of type 2 diabetes. It does not require special foods or giving up home-cooked Indian meals. It focuses on balance, portions and smarter choices.
Why does diet matter in prediabetes?
Carbohydrates have the biggest impact on blood sugar. Refined carbohydrates and sugar raise blood sugar quickly, while fibre, protein and healthy fats slow absorption. Better food choices also support weight loss, which improves insulin sensitivity.
What should you eat more of?
Vegetables
Leafy greens, gourds, beans, cauliflower, cabbage, okra and salads add fibre and nutrients with few calories.
Whole grains and millets
Jowar, bajra, ragi, broken wheat, brown rice and whole wheat atta digest more slowly than refined grains.
Pulses and legumes
Dal, rajma, chana, moong and sprouts provide protein and fibre.
Protein
Paneer, curd, eggs, fish, chicken and tofu help with fullness and steadier sugar.
Healthy fats in moderation
Nuts, seeds and small amounts of cooking oil such as mustard or groundnut oil.
Whole fruits
Choose whole fruits like guava, apple, papaya or orange in moderate portions rather than juices.
What should you limit?
Sugary drinks, packaged juices and sweetened tea or coffee
Sweets, mithai and desserts
Maida-based foods like white bread, biscuits, bakery items and noodles
Large portions of white rice and potatoes
Fried snacks such as samosas, pakoras and chips
Processed and packaged foods high in sugar or salt
How do you build a balanced plate?
Use the plate method:
Half: non-starchy vegetables and salad
Quarter: protein such as dal, paneer, fish or chicken
Quarter: whole grain roti, millet bhakri or a controlled portion of rice
What are simple swaps?
White rice to mixed rice with vegetables or millet
Maida roti to whole wheat or jowar roti
Fruit juice to whole fruit
Fried snacks to roasted chana or sprouts chaat
Sweetened tea to unsweetened or less sugar
Does meal timing matter?
Regular meals help prevent overeating and large sugar spikes. Avoid long gaps followed by heavy meals. A short walk after meals can help lower post-meal sugar.
A quick recap
You can keep Indian food while managing prediabetes.
Use the plate method for balance.
Choose whole grains, pulses, vegetables and protein.
Limit sugar, maida, fried foods and large rice portions.
Regular meals and walks after meals help.
Small, sustainable food changes can bring prediabetes back toward normal. A personalised plan works better than strict diets. Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar at Niramay Clinics in Nagpur helps patients build practical Indian meal plans for prediabetes and diabetes.
When to seek urgent care
Seek urgent care for extreme thirst with vomiting, confusion, deep rapid breathing or unusual drowsiness, which can signal very high sugar.

Frequently asked questions
What foods should I avoid with prediabetes?
Limit sugary drinks, sweets, maida-based bakery foods, fried snacks, large portions of white rice and processed foods. These raise blood sugar quickly. You do not need to avoid all carbohydrates, but choose whole grains and control portions.
Can I eat rice if I have prediabetes?
Yes, in controlled portions. Combine rice with dal, vegetables and protein, and consider mixing with millets or choosing brown rice. Keeping rice to about a quarter of your plate helps.
Which roti is best for prediabetes?
Whole wheat, jowar, bajra or ragi rotis are better choices than maida rotis because they contain more fibre. Portion size still matters.
Are fruits allowed in prediabetes?
Yes. Whole fruits such as guava, apple, papaya, orange and berries are good in moderate portions. Avoid fruit juices and limit very sweet fruits in large amounts.
Do I need a special diabetic diet for prediabetes?
No special foods are needed. A balanced Indian diet with portion control, more vegetables and protein, whole grains and fewer sweets and refined foods is effective. A dietitian can personalise it.
