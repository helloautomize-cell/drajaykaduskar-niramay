Quick answer
HbA1c is usually checked every three months when control is changing and every six months when it is stable. Home blood sugar testing may range from several times a day for people on insulin to a few times a week for those on tablets with stable control. Your doctor will set a schedule for you.
Testing shows how well your plan is working. The right frequency depends on your treatment, your control and your doctor's advice.
HbA1c testing
Every three months if treatment has changed or sugar is not at target
Every six months if control is stable
No fasting needed
Home blood sugar testing
On insulin
Often before meals and at bedtime, and when symptoms occur.
On tablets only
Often fasting and after meals a few days a week, as advised.
Newly diagnosed or changing treatment
More frequent testing for a period.
Useful times to test
Fasting, before breakfast
Two hours after a main meal
Before bed
When you feel unwell or have symptoms of low or high sugar
Keep a record
Write readings in a notebook or app with notes on meals and activity. Bring it to your visits.
Other regular checks
Blood pressure and cholesterol
Kidney tests
Eye and foot examinations
In short
HbA1c is checked every three to six months.
Home testing depends on treatment.
Test at useful times, not randomly.
Keep a record.
Follow your doctor's plan.
Testing is only useful when the results guide action. For a monitoring plan, you can consult Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar at Niramay Clinics in Nagpur.
When to seek urgent care
Seek urgent care for extreme thirst with vomiting, confusion, deep rapid breathing, unusual drowsiness, or signs of very low sugar such as sweating, shaking and confusion.

Frequently asked questions
Do I need to test daily if I take only tablets?
Not always. Your doctor will advise how often.
Why is my HbA1c high when my fasting sugar is normal?
Post-meal sugars may be high. Testing after meals can help.
Can I test at any time?
Yes, but readings are most useful at set times.
Is a glucometer accurate?
Reasonably, if used correctly. Lab tests are the reference.
Should I test when I feel unwell?
Yes, illness can raise sugar.
What if my readings are always high?
Contact your doctor, as treatment may need adjusting.
