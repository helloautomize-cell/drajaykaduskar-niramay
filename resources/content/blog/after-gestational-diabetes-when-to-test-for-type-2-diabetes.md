**Quick answer.** After gestational diabetes, get a glucose test 6 to 12 weeks after delivery, ideally a 75 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT). If the result is normal, repeat testing at least every 1 to 3 years for life. If it shows prediabetes, test yearly and start preventive steps. If it shows diabetes, begin treatment. Do not wait for symptoms.

Many women feel relief once their baby is born and the diabetes seems to have gone. But having had gestational diabetes means your body has shown a tendency to struggle with sugar. A simple testing schedule can catch problems early, long before they cause harm.

#### Why Is Follow-up Testing So Important?

Women with a history of gestational diabetes have a significantly higher lifetime risk of type 2 diabetes. Many develop it within five to ten years. In India, with high diabetes risk, this is even more relevant. Type 2 diabetes can be silent for years, and early detection prevents complications of the eyes, kidneys, nerves and heart.

#### What Tests Should You Do?

##### First test: 6 to 12 weeks after delivery

A 75 g OGTT is the preferred test. You fast overnight, give a sample, drink the glucose solution and give another sample at two hours. If you are breastfeeding, it is safe to do.

##### Later tests

- Fasting blood sugar

- HbA1c

- OGTT, if indicated

Your doctor will choose which test suits you. HbA1c may be less reliable in some situations, such as anaemia or recent blood loss around childbirth, so it is often delayed until several months after delivery.

#### How Do You Interpret the Results?

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| Fasting sugar below 100 mg/dL and 2-hour OGTT below 140 mg/dL | Normal |
| Fasting 100 to 125 mg/dL, or 2-hour 140 to 199 mg/dL | Prediabetes |
| Fasting 126 mg/dL or higher, or 2-hour 200 mg/dL or higher | Diabetes |
| HbA1c below 5.7 percent | Normal |
| HbA1c 5.7 to 6.4 percent | Prediabetes |
| HbA1c 6.5 percent or higher | Diabetes |

A diagnosis should be confirmed by your doctor.

#### How Often Should You Test?

- **Normal result:** At least every 3 years, or yearly if you have other risk factors.

- **Prediabetes:** Yearly, along with lifestyle changes. Your doctor may consider metformin if risk is very high.

- **Before another pregnancy:** For a baseline check and planning.

- **Any time:** If you notice symptoms such as excess thirst, frequent urination, tiredness or blurred vision.

#### How Can You Lower Your Risk?

- Aim for a healthy weight, including a gradual return to your pre-pregnancy weight.

- Follow a balanced diet with whole grains, pulses, vegetables and limited sweets.

- Be active for 150 minutes a week, including strength training.

- Breastfeed if you can, for as long as possible.

- Avoid smoking and limit alcohol.

- Sleep as well as you can, and manage stress.

- Check your blood pressure and cholesterol.

#### What If You Plan Another Pregnancy?

Visit your doctor before conceiving. Check your sugar, aim for a healthy weight and begin folic acid. Early testing is advised in the next pregnancy, since gestational diabetes can recur.

#### When Should You See a Doctor?

See your doctor if you have symptoms of high sugar, if you missed your post-delivery test or if your last test was more than a few years ago.

#### Key Takeaways

- Test 6 to 12 weeks after delivery, preferably with an OGTT.

- Repeat every 1 to 3 years for life, or yearly if prediabetes is found.

- Lifestyle changes sharply lower the risk of type 2 diabetes.

- Plan the next pregnancy early with your doctor.

- Do not wait for symptoms.

#### Frequently asked questions

**When should I test for diabetes after gestational diabetes?**

Get a test 6 to 12 weeks after delivery, preferably a 75 g OGTT. Then test every 1 to 3 years for life, or yearly if prediabetes is found.

**Which test is best after gestational diabetes?**

The OGTT is often preferred in the first test after delivery. Fasting sugar and HbA1c are used for later checks. Your doctor will choose the best test for you.

**Is HbA1c reliable right after pregnancy?**

It can be less reliable shortly after delivery, especially with anaemia or blood loss. Many doctors prefer OGTT in the early weeks and use HbA1c later.

**How likely am I to get type 2 diabetes?**

Your risk is higher than average, with studies showing several times higher risk. Lifestyle changes, healthy weight and regular testing greatly reduce it.

**Can breastfeeding lower my diabetes risk?**

Breastfeeding may help improve sugar metabolism and weight loss, and is linked with a lower risk of type 2 diabetes. It is encouraged where possible.

**Should I take metformin to prevent diabetes?**

In women with prediabetes after gestational diabetes and a high risk, doctors may consider metformin along with lifestyle change. The decision is individual.
