**Quick answer.** Aim for gradual weight loss using portion control, more vegetables and protein, fewer refined carbohydrates and sweets, and regular activity. Avoid crash diets and extreme fasting, especially if you take insulin or sugar-lowering medicines, as these can cause dangerously low sugar.

Weight loss can improve blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol. With diabetes, the way you lose weight matters as much as how much.

#### Build the Plate

- Half the plate: vegetables and salad

- A quarter: protein such as dal, eggs, fish, paneer or chicken

- A quarter: whole grains, millets or a controlled portion of rice or roti

#### Choices That Help

- Choose whole grains and millets over refined flour

- Add fibre through vegetables, pulses and fruit in portions

- Prefer baking, steaming and grilling over deep frying

- Limit sweets, sugary drinks and packaged snacks

- Eat at regular times and avoid long gaps

#### Portions and Habits

- Use a smaller plate

- Eat slowly

- Avoid eating in front of a screen

- Keep healthy snacks like roasted chana or nuts in small amounts

#### Move More

Brisk walking, strength exercise and an active day support weight loss and sugar control.

#### Safety With Medicines

If you take insulin or tablets that can cause low sugar, discuss diet changes with your doctor first, as doses may need adjusting.

#### Realistic Pace

A steady loss over weeks to months is easier to sustain than rapid loss.

#### Key Takeaways

- Gradual loss is safest.

- Fill half the plate with vegetables.

- Cut refined carbohydrates and sweets.

- Adjust medicines with medical advice.

- Combine diet with activity.

#### Final Thoughts

A diet you can keep up is the one that works. For a personalised plan, you can consult Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar at Niramay Clinics in Nagpur.

#### When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent care for vomiting, confusion, unusual drowsiness or symptoms of very high or very low sugar, especially if you take insulin or tablets that can cause low sugar.

#### Frequently asked questions

**Can I lose weight and still eat rice?**

Yes, in controlled portions with vegetables and protein.

**Are keto or very low carb diets safe with diabetes?**

They can be risky with some medicines. Speak to your doctor first.

**Is intermittent fasting safe?**

It may not be safe with insulin or certain tablets. Get medical advice.

**How much weight should I lose?**

Even a modest loss helps. Your doctor can set a target.

**Do I need to count calories?**

Not necessarily. Portion control and plate structure work well for many people.

**Can I eat fruit?**

Yes, in controlled portions, preferably whole fruit.
