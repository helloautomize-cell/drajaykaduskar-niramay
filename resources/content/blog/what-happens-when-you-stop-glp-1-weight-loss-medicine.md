**Quick answer.** When GLP-1 medicines are stopped, appetite usually returns and a large portion of lost weight is often regained over the next months. Blood sugar may also rise in people with diabetes. The medicine controls appetite while you take it, but it does not permanently reset the body. A planned, doctor-guided approach, with lifestyle support, can reduce regain.

Many people start a GLP-1 medicine and lose weight, then wonder what happens when they stop. The honest answer is that for most people, appetite and weight tend to return. Understanding why helps you plan, instead of being surprised.

#### Why does weight come back?

Obesity is a chronic condition. The body defends its higher weight with hormones that increase hunger and reduce fullness. GLP-1 medicines lower these signals. Once the medicine leaves the body, hunger signals return to their previous levels. Studies that followed people after stopping have shown that, on average, most of the weight lost was regained within about a year.

#### What else changes after stopping?

- **Appetite and cravings:** Often return within weeks.

- **Blood sugar:** In type 2 diabetes, HbA1c may rise again.

- **Blood pressure and cholesterol:** Improvements may fade as weight returns.

- **Fatty liver and sleep apnoea:** May worsen if weight returns.

- **Side effects:** Nausea and stomach symptoms stop.

#### Does everyone regain?

Not everyone. People who build strong habits during treatment, such as regular exercise, protein-rich meals, good sleep and mindful eating, tend to keep more of the benefit. Some people maintain with a lower dose. Others may restart later.

#### How can you plan to stop safely?

##### Talk to your doctor first

Do not stop suddenly without advice, especially if you have diabetes and take other medicines. Your doctor may adjust other medicines to avoid high sugar.

##### Taper or reduce the dose

In some cases, doctors lower the dose in steps, rather than stopping at once, though evidence for the best approach is still developing.

##### Build habits during treatment

- Keep protein at every meal.

- Do strength exercises two to three times a week.

- Walk daily.

- Sleep seven to eight hours.

- Learn portion sizes and mindful eating.

- Limit sweet drinks and ultra-processed snacks.

##### Monitor after stopping

Weigh yourself weekly and check your waist. For diabetes, monitor sugar levels and have an HbA1c test in about three months. Early action on small regain is easier than a big regain.

##### Consider long-term options

Some people stay on a lower maintenance dose. Others use different medicines, or consider metabolic surgery if severe obesity returns. Your doctor can discuss these.

#### When might you stop?

- You have reached your goal and have a maintenance plan

- Side effects are not tolerable

- A medical condition makes it unsafe

- You are planning pregnancy

- You cannot afford to continue

Always plan this with your doctor.

#### When should you see a doctor?

See your doctor if you notice rapid weight regain, high sugar readings, increased thirst or urination, or return of symptoms such as snoring or knee pain after you stop.

#### In short

- Appetite and weight usually return after stopping a GLP-1 medicine.

- Blood sugar may rise again in type 2 diabetes.

- Habits built during treatment help keep results.

- Do not stop suddenly without medical advice.

- Plan maintenance, monitoring and next steps with your doctor.

#### Frequently asked questions

**Will I regain weight after stopping GLP-1 injections?**

Many people do. Studies show that on average a large share of lost weight returns within a year, because appetite signals go back to their earlier levels. Lifestyle habits and maintenance plans can reduce regain.

**Can I stop a GLP-1 medicine suddenly?**

It is not harmful in the way some medicines are, but sudden stopping can lead to quick return of appetite and high blood sugar in diabetes. Talk to your doctor before stopping.

**How long after stopping does hunger return?**

Many people notice increased appetite within a few weeks as the medicine leaves the body. The timing varies with the medicine and dose.

**Can I keep my weight off after stopping?**

Some can, especially with strong habits such as strength training, protein-rich meals, good sleep and regular follow-up. Others need a lower maintenance dose or a different treatment.

**Will my diabetes come back if I stop?**

If you have type 2 diabetes, blood sugar often rises again after stopping, since the underlying condition remains. Your doctor may adjust other medicines to keep control.

**Is a lower maintenance dose possible?**

Some doctors use lower doses to maintain results, but the best approach depends on the person and the medicine. It should be decided with your doctor.
