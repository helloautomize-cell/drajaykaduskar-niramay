**Quick answer.** Even a modest loss of around 5 percent of body weight can improve blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol. Greater and sustained weight loss improves control further, and in some people, especially soon after diagnosis, it can lead to remission. The right pace and plan should be set with your doctor.

In type 2 diabetes, excess body fat makes the body less responsive to insulin. Losing weight can reverse part of that problem.

#### Why Weight Loss Helps

- Reduces fat in the liver and pancreas

- Improves insulin sensitivity

- Lowers blood pressure and cholesterol

- May reduce the dose or number of medicines

#### What Different Amounts May Do

- Modest loss: better sugar and blood pressure

- Larger loss: bigger improvement and, for some, remission

- Maintaining the loss matters as much as achieving it

#### Safe Ways to Lose Weight

- Portion control and fewer refined carbohydrates

- More vegetables, pulses and protein

- Regular activity, including strength work

- Good sleep

- Medical support when needed, including medicines or procedures

#### Adjust Medicines

As weight falls, sugar may drop. Doses of insulin and some tablets may need reducing to avoid low sugar. Do not change them yourself.

#### Keep the Weight Off

- Continue healthy habits

- Weigh yourself regularly

- Keep follow-up visits

#### Key Takeaways

- Modest weight loss helps.

- More loss can bring greater benefit.

- Medicines may need adjusting.

- Sustained habits keep results.

- Medical support is available.

#### When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent care for chest pain, breathlessness, confusion, vomiting, or symptoms of very high or very low sugar.

#### Frequently asked questions

**Can weight loss reverse diabetes?**

In some people, especially early in the disease, it can lead to remission.

**How fast should I lose weight?**

Gradual loss is safer and easier to maintain.

**Do I stop my medicines when I lose weight?**

Only your doctor should change doses.

**What if I cannot lose weight?**

Speak to your doctor about other options.

**Does belly fat matter?**

Yes, abdominal fat is closely linked to insulin resistance.

**Is exercise enough?**

It helps, but combining with diet works better.
