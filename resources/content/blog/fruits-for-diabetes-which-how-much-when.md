**Quick answer.** People with diabetes can eat most fruits in moderate portions, usually one small to medium fruit or about one cup of chopped fruit at a time. Good choices include guava, apple, pear, orange, papaya, berries and jamun. Eat fruit whole rather than as juice, pair it with protein or nuts, and limit very sweet fruits such as mango, chikoo and grapes to small portions.

A common myth is that people with diabetes must avoid fruit. In reality, whole fruits provide fibre, vitamins and antioxidants and can be part of a healthy diabetes diet. The key is choosing suitable fruits, controlling portions and timing them sensibly.

#### Why Is Whole Fruit Better Than Juice?

Whole fruit contains fibre that slows sugar absorption and keeps you full. Juice removes fibre and concentrates sugar, raising blood sugar quickly. Even fresh juice without added sugar is best avoided or limited.

#### Which Fruits Are Good Choices?

##### Lower to moderate impact fruits

- Guava

- Apple

- Pear

- Orange and sweet lime

- Papaya

- Berries such as strawberries

- Jamun

- Peach and plum

##### Fruits to eat in smaller portions

- Mango

- Banana, especially ripe

- Chikoo (sapota)

- Grapes

- Custard apple

- Pineapple

These are not forbidden but contain more sugar per portion.

##### Dried fruits

Raisins, dates and figs are concentrated sources of sugar. Small amounts may fit occasionally, but portions should be very small.

#### How Much Fruit Is Right?

Most people do well with one portion at a time and two to three portions a day, depending on their overall diet and sugar control. A portion is roughly one small apple, one orange, one guava or about a cup of papaya. Your doctor or dietitian can personalise this.

#### When Is the Best Time to Eat Fruit?

- As a mid-morning or evening snack

- With a source of protein or healthy fat, such as a few nuts or curd

- Not as a large dessert straight after a heavy carbohydrate meal

- Avoid eating several fruits at once

#### Do Fruit Myths Matter?

Fruit does not need to be eaten only on an empty stomach, and no single fruit cures diabetes. Balance and portion size matter most.

#### How Can You Check Your Response?

Checking blood sugar about two hours after eating fruit helps you understand how your body responds to different fruits.

#### Key Takeaways

- People with diabetes can eat fruit.

- Choose whole fruits over juice.

- Guava, apple, orange, papaya and berries are good choices.

- Limit very sweet fruits and dried fruits.

- Pair fruit with protein or nuts and control portions.

#### Final Thoughts

Fruit can be a healthy, enjoyable part of a diabetes diet when chosen and portioned wisely. Personal monitoring helps you find what works for you. Dr. Ajay V. Kaduskar at Niramay Clinics in Nagpur guides patients on practical diabetes nutrition.

#### When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent care for vomiting, confusion, unusual drowsiness or symptoms of very high or very low sugar, especially if you take insulin or tablets that can cause low sugar.

#### Frequently asked questions

**Which fruits are best for diabetes?**

Guava, apple, pear, orange, papaya, berries and jamun are good choices because they have fibre and a moderate effect on blood sugar. Portion size is still important.

**Can diabetics eat mango?**

Mango can be eaten occasionally in a small portion, such as a few slices, ideally with protein or nuts and not after a heavy meal. Monitor your sugar to see how you respond.

**Is fruit juice okay for diabetes?**

Fruit juice raises blood sugar quickly because it lacks fibre and concentrates sugar. Whole fruit is a much better choice. Limit or avoid juices.

**How many fruits can a diabetic eat per day?**

Many people do well with two to three portions spread through the day. Your doctor or dietitian can personalise this based on your diet and sugar control.

**Can diabetics eat dates and dry fruits?**

Dried fruits like dates and raisins are high in concentrated sugar. Very small portions may be eaten occasionally, but nuts such as almonds and walnuts are better daily snack choices.
