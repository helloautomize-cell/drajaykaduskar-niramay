**Quick answer.** Excess body fat, especially around the abdomen, causes insulin resistance, which can lead to type 2 diabetes. Diabetes and some of its medicines can in turn make weight gain easier. Managing both through food, activity, sleep, suitable medicines and sometimes surgery can improve sugar, weight and overall health.

"Diabesity" is an informal term for diabetes that occurs alongside obesity. The two conditions feed each other, so treating them together works best.

#### How the Two Are Linked

- Fat cells release substances that reduce insulin action

- The pancreas works harder and may tire over time

- High insulin levels promote fat storage

- Some diabetes medicines can add weight

#### Why South Asians Need Extra Care

People of South Asian origin may develop diabetes at lower body weights and with more abdominal fat, so waist size matters.

#### Health Risks

- Heart disease and high blood pressure

- Fatty liver

- Sleep apnoea

- Joint problems

- Kidney and eye complications

#### Managing Both Together

- Balanced diet with portion control

- Regular physical activity

- Good sleep

- Medicines that help both sugar and weight, chosen by your doctor

- Metabolic surgery for selected people with significant obesity

#### Set Realistic Goals

Steady weight loss and improving blood sugar are goals worth celebrating, even without a "perfect" weight.

#### Key Takeaways

- Diabetes and obesity are closely linked.

- Waist size matters, especially for South Asians.

- Treating both gives better results.

- Medicines and surgery are options.

- Realistic goals sustain progress.

#### When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent care for chest pain, breathlessness, confusion, vomiting, or symptoms of very high or very low sugar.

#### Frequently asked questions

**Is diabesity a medical diagnosis?**

No, it is an informal term describing the overlap.

**Can I have diabetes without being overweight?**

Yes, especially in South Asians.

**Does losing weight help diabetes?**

Yes, it often improves control.

**Do all diabetes medicines cause weight gain?**

No. Some are weight-neutral and some help weight loss.

**Is surgery an option?**

For selected people with obesity and diabetes, yes.

**Where should I start?**

With a medical assessment and a realistic plan.
