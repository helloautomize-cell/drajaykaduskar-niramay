**Quick answer.** Weight loss is harder in type 2 diabetes because of insulin resistance, high insulin levels that encourage fat storage, stronger hunger signals, some diabetes medicines that cause weight gain, poor sleep and stress, and a body that defends its weight. The right combination of food, activity, sleep, medicine adjustments and, when needed, weight-loss medicines can overcome these barriers.

If you have type 2 diabetes and find that weight does not budge despite effort, you are not alone, and you are not lazy. Diabetes, its medicines and the hormones involved can all work against weight loss. Knowing why helps you choose smarter strategies.

#### What Makes It Harder?

##### 1. Insulin resistance and high insulin levels

When cells resist insulin, the body makes more of it. High insulin promotes fat storage, especially around the belly, and makes fat harder to burn.

##### 2. Hunger and fullness hormones

Obesity and diabetes can change hormones such as leptin, ghrelin and GLP-1. Hunger rises, fullness fades, and cravings increase.

##### 3. Medicines that raise weight

Insulin, sulfonylureas (such as glimepiride) and pioglitazone can cause weight gain. Some medicines for blood pressure, depression, epilepsy or steroids can also add weight. Never stop them on your own. Ask your doctor about alternatives.

##### 4. Fear of low blood sugar

Some people eat extra snacks to avoid hypoglycaemia, which adds calories.

##### 5. Poor sleep and stress

Short sleep and stress increase appetite and cortisol, which favours belly fat.

##### 6. Reduced muscle and activity

Fatigue, joint pain, neuropathy or foot problems can limit exercise. Less muscle lowers the metabolic rate.

##### 7. Other conditions

Thyroid disease, PCOS, sleep apnoea and depression may make weight loss harder.

##### 8. Crash diets and yo-yo cycles

Extreme dieting leads to muscle loss, slower metabolism and rebound weight gain.

#### What Actually Helps?

##### Food

- Eat balanced plates: half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter whole grains.

- Reduce sweets, sugary drinks, fried food and refined flour.

- Eat protein at each meal to protect muscle and control hunger.

- Eat slowly, and avoid skipping meals.

- Keep a food diary for two weeks to find hidden calories.

##### Movement

- Walk after meals for 10 to 15 minutes.

- Aim for 150 minutes or more of weekly exercise.

- Add resistance training twice or thrice a week.

- Reduce long sitting.

##### Sleep and stress

Prioritise seven to eight hours of sleep, treat sleep apnoea and find ways to relax.

##### Medicine review

Ask your doctor whether your diabetes medicines can be changed to ones that help weight, such as metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 medicines.

##### Medical and surgical options

GLP-1 medicines, other weight-loss medicines and metabolic surgery can be considered in suitable patients with obesity.

#### How Much Weight Loss Is Worth Aiming For?

Even 5 percent can improve blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol. Ten percent or more can improve blood sugar dramatically, and in some cases lead to remission of diabetes.

#### What Should You Avoid?

- Crash diets and fasting without medical advice

- Skipping medicines

- Weight-loss supplements and unverified products

- Comparing yourself with others

#### When Should You See a Doctor?

See a diabetologist if you are gaining weight despite effort, if your sugars are not controlled, or if you plan major dietary changes while taking insulin or tablets that can cause low sugar.

#### Key Takeaways

- Insulin resistance, hormones, medicines, sleep and stress can make weight loss harder in type 2 diabetes.

- It is a biological issue, not a lack of willpower.

- Balanced eating, activity, sleep and medicine review help.

- Weight-loss medicines or surgery can be options.

- Even 5 percent weight loss brings benefits.

#### Frequently asked questions

**Why can't I lose weight with type 2 diabetes?**

High insulin levels, insulin resistance, hormones, certain medicines, poor sleep and stress can make it harder. A structured plan and medicine review often help.

**Does insulin make you gain weight?**

It can, because insulin promotes fat storage and some people eat more to prevent low sugar. Careful dose adjustment, diet, exercise and sometimes adding other medicines can limit gain.

**Which diabetes medicines help with weight loss?**

Metformin may be weight-neutral or lead to slight loss. SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 medicines can help reduce weight. Your doctor will choose based on your health.

**How much weight should I aim to lose?**

Start with 5 percent of your body weight, then aim for 10 percent or more if appropriate. Even a modest loss improves blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol.

**Is exercise enough to lose weight in diabetes?**

Exercise helps blood sugar and fitness, but weight loss usually needs diet changes too. Combine regular activity with healthy eating and good sleep.

**Can diabetes go into remission with weight loss?**

In some people, substantial weight loss, especially early in the disease, can lead to remission. It needs medical follow-up because diabetes can return if weight comes back.
