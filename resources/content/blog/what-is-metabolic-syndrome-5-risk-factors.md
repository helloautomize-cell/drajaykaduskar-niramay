**Quick answer.** Metabolic syndrome is usually diagnosed when a person has at least three of five risk factors: a large waist, high blood pressure, high blood sugar, high triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol. Lifestyle changes, such as weight loss, activity and a healthier diet, can improve all of them.

Metabolic syndrome is not a single disease. It is a group of related problems that often appear together and increase the risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease and stroke.

#### The Five Risk Factors

##### 1. Large waist

Excess fat around the abdomen. Cut-offs are lower for South Asians than for many other groups.

##### 2. High blood pressure

Blood pressure above the target range, or treatment for it.

##### 3. High blood sugar

Raised fasting sugar or treatment for diabetes.

##### 4. High triglycerides

Raised levels of this blood fat.

##### 5. Low HDL cholesterol

Low levels of the "good" cholesterol.

#### Why It Matters

Together these factors raise the risk of diabetes, heart attack, stroke and fatty liver.

#### How It Is Diagnosed

Measurements of waist, blood pressure and blood tests for sugar and lipids. Your doctor interprets them together.

#### How to Reduce the Risk

- Lose excess weight, especially abdominal fat

- Exercise regularly

- Eat more vegetables, whole grains and pulses

- Cut sugar, refined carbohydrates and fried foods

- Stop smoking and limit alcohol

- Treat blood pressure, sugar and cholesterol as advised

#### Key Takeaways

- Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of risk factors.

- Three of five make the diagnosis.

- It raises diabetes and heart risk.

- Lifestyle change helps all components.

- Early action matters.

Seek urgent care for chest pain, breathlessness, confusion, vomiting, or symptoms of very high or very low sugar.

#### Frequently asked questions

**Is metabolic syndrome the same as diabetes?**

No, but it greatly raises the risk of developing it.

**Can it be reversed?**

Many people improve with weight loss and healthier habits.

**Do I need medicines?**

Some people do for blood pressure, sugar or cholesterol.

**Does it cause symptoms?**

Usually not, apart from signs such as a larger waist.

**Can thin people have it?**

Yes. Some people with a normal weight still have the risk factors.

**How often should I be checked?**

Your doctor will advise based on your risk.
