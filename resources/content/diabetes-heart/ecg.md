**H1:** ECG (electrocardiogram)

An ECG records the electrical signals of your heart through small sticky pads placed on the chest, arms and legs. It takes about 5 to 10 minutes and needs no preparation; you will feel nothing more than the sticky pads.

#### What an ECG can show
- Heart rate and rhythm, including irregular rhythms such as atrial fibrillation
- Signs of a previous heart attack, or strain on the heart
- Thickening of the heart muscle
- Effects of some medicines and of potassium or other salt imbalances

#### When it is done
As part of diabetes and [blood pressure](/hypertension-clinic/) check-ups, before some treatments, for palpitations, dizziness or chest discomfort, and in preventive health check-ups.

#### Good to know
A normal resting ECG does not rule out all heart disease, because some problems appear only during exertion. Your doctor may then advise a [treadmill](/heart-care/tmt-stress-test/) test or Echo.

**If you have chest pain right now, do not book an ECG. Call 108 or 112 or go to the nearest emergency department.**

#### When to see a doctor
An ECG is routine, but chest pain or pressure, sudden breathlessness, fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat with dizziness, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back needs urgent attention - call 108 or 112 rather than booking a routine test.
#### Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an appointment for an ECG?**
An appointment helps us keep your waiting time short, but an ECG is quick and we try to fit in patients who walk in during OPD hours. Call ahead if you are in a hurry.

**Is an ECG safe?**
Yes. An ECG only records your heart's electrical signals through sticky pads on the skin. Nothing enters the body, there is no radiation, and it is safe in pregnancy and for children.

**How long does an ECG take?**
The recording itself takes a few minutes. Including preparation and a short discussion of the report, plan for about 15 to 20 minutes at the clinic.

**When will I get my ECG report?**
The tracing is available almost immediately and Dr. Ajay usually reviews it during the same visit, so you leave with the report and its meaning explained.
